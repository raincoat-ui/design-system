3.4

Card

A surface that groups content about one subject, and can lead somewhere as a whole.

Spec version 1.0.0, proposed in RFC 0005.

Implemented in React (Card, CardMedia, CardTitle, CardLink, CardButton, CardBody, and CardFooter in @raincoat-ui/react), SwiftUI (RaincoatCard and RaincoatCardStyle in Raincoat), and Compose (RaincoatCard in io.github.raincoat-ui:ui). The parity matrix shows every component on every platform.

About the renditions

The panels under “In every color mode” are static renditions, not the component: each interactive card is drawn in one state, and nothing in them can be activated. They are styled only with the custom properties of the generated theme stylesheet, and each panel selects its mode with a data-theme attribute.

Properties

Section titled “Properties”
Property Values Default Description
interactive true, false false Makes the whole card lead to one place or do one thing, whichever part of it is activated: a link when it has a destination, and a button when it has an action. An interactive card holds no other controls. A static card, the default, is a container that can hold links and buttons.

Content

Section titled “Content”
Content Description Required
Title What the card is about, such as an order or a person. It is a heading in heading-sm, at the level the app gives, from 1 to 6, for the card’s place in the outline of the screen. It wraps and is never cut short. Yes
Media An image at the top of the card. Its text alternative comes from the app, and is empty when the image only decorates. No
Body Any content, such as text, a list of facts, or a status. No
Footer Actions, as Buttons, at the end of a static card. No
Destination Where an interactive card leads, which makes it a link. For a card that is a link
Action What an interactive card does, which makes it a button. For a card that is a button

States

Section titled “States”
State Description
Rest The default appearance. An interactive card at rest looks like a static one.
Hover A pointer is over an interactive card. The outline steps to the strong border color. Exists only where there is a pointer.
Focus visible An interactive card has keyboard focus. The focus ring is shown around the whole card.
Pressed An interactive card is being pressed. The fill steps to the control hover color, and the outline to the strong border color.

Part Description Tokens

Accessibility

Section titled “Accessibility”
Requirement Detail
Role A static card has no role of its own: it is a group of content whose title is a heading. An interactive card is a link when it has a destination and a button when it has an action. Android has no link role, so there a card that leads somewhere is a button, as a row that opens a screen is.
Name The title. An interactive card is named by its title alone, and the rest of the card is read after it.
Heading The title is a heading at the level the app gives, on every platform, so people can move from card to card by heading.
Focus A ring at least 2px wide with 3:1 contrast against the surface behind the card, around the whole card and 2px from its edge, shown for keyboard focus. On the web and on Android the card draws it from the focus ring tokens. On iOS the system draws its own focus indicator, which follows the person’s Full Keyboard Access settings.
Collections A card has no role that counts it. Put a collection of cards in a list, so that a screen reader says how many there are.
Target size An interactive card takes touches across its whole surface.
Contrast Text keeps 4.5:1 on the fill at rest and pressed, and 7:1 in high-contrast modes. The strong outline of hover and press has 3:1 against the surfaces a card sits on. The outline at rest is decorative: the content of a card identifies it.

Keyboard

Section titled “Keyboard”
Keys Action
Tab Moves focus to and from an interactive card, which is one stop. A static card takes no focus itself: Tab moves to the links and buttons inside it.
Enter Activates an interactive card.
Space Activates an interactive card that is a button.

Internationalization

Section titled “Internationalization”
Concern Behavior
Right to left Every part aligns to the start edge, which is the right, and footer actions sit at the end, on the left. Media does not flip: it is a picture, not a direction.
Long text The title and body wrap and the card grows taller. Nothing is truncated. In a row of cards, a card with longer text grows rather than clips.
Scripts The title is a Heading, whose line height clears stacked Vietnamese diacritics. Chinese, Japanese, and Korean use system fonts.

Motion

Section titled “Motion”
Concern Behavior
Transitions Outline and fill changes of an interactive card use card.transition.duration and its easing.
Reduced motion Outline and fill changes are immediate.

Guidelines

Section titled “Guidelines”

When to use

Section titled “When to use”

Use a card to group content about one subject, such as an order, a contact, or an article, when a screen shows several of them for people to choose between. For a single block of content on a screen, use a section with a heading: a card adds an outline and nothing else.

Static or interactive

Section titled “Static or interactive”
Kind Use it when Holds
Static The card shows content, or offers more than one thing to do Any content, links, and Buttons
Interactive The whole card leads to one place, or does one thing Content only: no links, Buttons, or fields
  • Make a card interactive only when the whole card leads to one place. If it needs a Button, keep it static and make the title the link.
  • An interactive card is a link when it leads somewhere, and a button when it does something on the same screen, such as opening a sheet. Choose by what happens, not by how it looks.
  • An interactive card holds no other controls. A control inside it would be a second target that a keyboard, a screen reader, and a touch each reach differently on each platform.
  • Do not disable a card. A card that cannot be opened should not look as if it could: make it static.

The title

Section titled “The title”
  • Give every card a title, even when the media seems to say it all. The title names the card for assistive technology, and people move from card to card by heading.
  • Choose the title’s level for the card’s place in the outline of the screen: the level after the heading of the section the cards are in. The look stays heading-sm.
  • Let titles wrap. Never cut a title short.

Media

Section titled “Media”
  • Media is an image at the top of the card, edge to edge. Give it a text alternative that says what the image shows, or an empty one when it only decorates.
  • Do not put text in the image. It does not translate, grow, or reach a screen reader.

Body and footer

Section titled “Body and footer”
  • Keep the body to what people need to choose this card over the others.
  • Shorten a description with max-lines (Typography) only when the full text is one tap away.
  • Put actions in the footer, as Buttons, only on a static card. They sit at the end of the card, and wrap when they do not fit.

Collections

Section titled “Collections”
  • Put a collection of cards in a list, so that a screen reader says how many there are: a ul or ol on the web.
  • A card fills the width it is given. The grid or list around it, and the space between cards, are the app’s.
  • In a row of cards, let each card grow with its content. Do not give cards a fixed height.

Do and don’t

Section titled “Do and don’t”
Do Don’t
Make the title the link of a static card that also has Buttons Put a Button inside an interactive card
Give every card a title Rely on an image to say what a card is
Let titles and bodies wrap, and cards grow Truncate a title, or give cards a fixed height
Keep every card in an app alike Change a card’s outline or fill to make it stand out
