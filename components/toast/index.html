3.2

Toast

A short message near the bottom of the screen that says something has happened.

Spec version 1.0.0, proposed in RFC 0008.

Implemented in React (ToastProvider, useToast, and Toast in @raincoat-ui/react), SwiftUI (RaincoatToast, raincoatToastHost, and showRaincoatToast in Raincoat), and Compose (RaincoatToastHost, RaincoatToastHostState, and RaincoatToast in io.github.raincoat-ui:ui). The parity matrix shows every component on every platform.

About the renditions

The panels under “In every color mode” are static renditions of a toast standing still, not the component: they are not announced, do not close, and their buttons are pictures. They are styled only with the custom properties of the generated theme stylesheet, and each panel selects its mode with a data-theme attribute.

Properties

Section titled “Properties”
Property Values Default Description
action true, false false Offers one action, such as Undo. A toast with an action also has a close button, and it stays until one of them is used.

Content

Section titled “Content”
Content Description Required
Message What happened, in one short sentence, such as “Address deleted”. It wraps and is never truncated. Yes
Action label The label of the action, in one or two words, such as “Undo” or “View”. When the toast has an action
Close label The name of the close button, read by assistive technology, such as “Close”. The button shows a cross drawn by the component. When the toast has an action, or a close button without one

States

Section titled “States”
State Description
Entering The toast slides up a short way while it fades in. With reduced motion it only fades.
Shown The toast is on the screen. Without an action, its timer runs; it closes when the timer ends.
Paused The timer is stopped while a pointer is over the toast, while focus is in it, and while the app is in the background. It goes on from where it stopped.
Leaving The toast fades out. The next toast in the queue, if any, enters after it.

Accessibility

Section titled “Accessibility”
Requirement Detail
Role A status message. On the web, the toasts are in a region landmark that the app names, and each message is announced through a status live region.
Name The message. The action is named by its label, and the close button by the name the app gives.
Announcement The message is announced once, politely: after what the screen reader is saying, without interrupting it. On the web through a status live region, on iOS as a VoiceOver announcement with low priority, and on Android as a polite live region.
Focus Never moved to the toast. Touch screen readers find it in the reading order, like any other element.
Timing Without an action, a toast closes after 6 seconds. The timer pauses while a pointer is over the toast, while it has focus, and while the app is in the background. On Android, the person’s accessibility timeout (“Time to take action”) replaces the 6 seconds when it is longer. A toast with an action never closes by itself (WCAG 2.2.1).
Closing The close button, Escape while the toast has focus, or a swipe down on a touch screen.
Contrast The message has 4.5:1 against the fill, and 7:1 in high-contrast modes. The outline has 3:1 against the fill and against the page.

Keyboard

Section titled “Keyboard”
Keys Action
F8 Moves focus to the toasts on the web, and F8 again from there moves it back.
Tab Moves between the toast, its action, and its close button once focus is in the toasts.
Escape Closes the toast that has focus.
Enter or Space Activates the action or the close button.

Internationalization

Section titled “Internationalization”
Concern Behavior
Right to left The action and close button sit at the end edge, on the left. The toast is centered, so its position does not change.
Long text The message wraps and the toast grows taller. When the message and the action do not fit on one line, the action moves below the message, at the end. Nothing is truncated.
Scripts Line height 1.5 clears stacked Vietnamese diacritics. CJK text uses system fonts.
Timing A translated message can take longer to read. The pause on hover and focus covers that; a message that needs more than 6 seconds is too long for a toast.

Motion

Section titled “Motion”
Concern Behavior
Enter Slides up from the toast margin while it fades in, with toast.enter.duration and its easing.
Exit Fades out and slides back down, with toast.exit.duration and its easing.
Reduced motion The toast fades in and out and does not move.

Guidelines

Section titled “Guidelines”

When to use

Section titled “When to use”

Use a toast to confirm an action whose result is not visible on the screen: an item archived, a link copied, a change saved in the background. It does not take focus or block the screen, so the person can go on with what they were doing.

Use a toast only for something people can also see or find elsewhere. A toast is gone in seconds and cannot be read again.

When not to use

Section titled “When not to use”
  • For an error that needs action. Put the message next to the thing that failed, or in a dialog, where it stays.
  • For anything that needs a decision. Use a dialog.
  • For a message longer than one sentence.
  • For an action whose result is already visible on the screen.

Writing the message

Section titled “Writing the message”
  • Say what happened in a short sentence: “Address deleted”, not “Success!”.
  • Name the thing, not the system: “Link copied”, not “Copied to clipboard successfully”.
  • Use sentence case.

The action

Section titled “The action”
  • Offer an action only to undo or to go to what was made.
  • Label it with one or two words: “Undo”, “View”.
  • A toast with an action stays until the action is used or the toast is closed, so nobody loses an Undo to a timer. It always has a close button.
  • Give the close button a name in the app’s language, such as “Close”. It shows only a cross.

One look

Section titled “One look”

There is one toast: no info, success, warning, or error variants. Color does not carry a status, and red is kept for danger. A toast reports something that has already happened.

Timing and order

Section titled “Timing and order”
  • A toast without an action closes after 6 seconds. The timer pauses while a pointer is over it, while it has focus, and while the app is in the background. On Android, the person’s accessibility timeout replaces the 6 seconds when it is longer.
  • One toast is shown at a time. Others wait in a queue and follow in order. Do not show a toast for every step of a long task; show one when it is done.

Placement

Section titled “Placement”

A toast sits at the bottom center of the window, above the on-screen keyboard. Tell the toast host about any bar the app keeps at the bottom of the screen, so the toast sits above it:

  • On the web, give ToastProvider the bar’s height as bottomInset.
  • On iOS, declare the bar with safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom) outside the host, or put the host inside the tab.
  • On Android, place RaincoatToastHost above the bar, as you would a snackbar host.

With a dialog or sheet

Section titled “With a dialog or sheet”

On the web, a toast shown while a dialog or sheet is open appears above it. On iOS and Android it appears on the screen behind, where the dialog covers it and its buttons cannot be reached. This is tracked in issue 68.

  • When an action closes the dialog, close it, then show the toast. On Android, show it from the screen’s coroutine scope: a scope remembered in the dialog ends when the dialog closes, and the toast with it.
  • When the dialog stays open, show the result in the dialog, such as a button that reads “Link copied”.
  • Do not put a toast host inside a dialog or sheet.

Do and don’t

Section titled “Do and don’t”
Do Don’t
“Message archived”, with “Undo” “Success! Your message was archived.”
One toast when a batch of work is done A toast for each item of the batch
Show a failure where it can be fixed Show “Upload failed” in a toast that disappears
Let a long message wrap Cut a message short with an ellipsis
