3.7

Typography

Sets text in the theme’s text styles, and marks headings for assistive technology.

Spec version 1.0.1, proposed in RFC 0003. The TextVariant, TextColor, HeadingVariant, and HeadingColor types used by every platform’s implementation are generated from this spec.

Implemented in React (Text and Heading in @raincoat-ui/react), SwiftUI (.raincoatText(_:) and .raincoatHeading(level:) in Raincoat), and Compose (RaincoatText and RaincoatHeading in io.github.raincoat-ui:ui). The parity matrix shows every component on every platform.

About the renditions

The text on this page under “Text styles” and “In every color mode” is a static rendition, not the component. Each line is styled only with the custom properties of the generated theme stylesheet, and each panel under “In every color mode” selects its mode with a data-theme attribute. The lines are paragraphs, not headings, so that they stay out of the page’s outline.

Properties

Section titled “Properties”

Text

Section titled “Text”

Running text and labels, in a body, label, or caption style.

Property Values Default Description
variant body-lg, body-md, body-sm, label-lg, label-md, label-sm, caption body-md The text style.
color default, muted, danger default The color of the text, from the theme’s text roles.
max-lines A whole number from 1 None The most lines the text shows. Text beyond them ends in an ellipsis, and assistive technology still reads all of it. Unset, the text wraps onto as many lines as it needs.

Part Description Tokens

type.label.*
type.caption
type.heading.*
color.fg.default

Ellipsis Ends Text that max-lines cuts short. A heading is never cut short, and neither is Text without max-lines.

Accessibility

Section titled “Accessibility”
Requirement Detail
Role Text has no role: assistive technology reads it as text. A Heading is a heading, with its level on the web and on iOS. Android has no heading levels, so there it is a heading without one.
Name The text itself. Text cut short by max-lines is read in full.
Text size Text grows with the person’s setting: the browser’s font size and zoom, Dynamic Type, and Android’s font scale. On iOS each style grows with the system text style nearest its role: heading-xl with large title, heading-lg with title 2, heading-md with title 3, heading-sm with headline, body and label styles with body, and caption with caption, so large headings grow less than body text, as the system’s own do. Android grows large text less than small text by itself. A heading is never smaller than the heading one level below it, at any text size: on iOS, where the large title grows least, a heading grows to at least the size of the look below it. At twice the default size nothing is clipped or overlaps.
Contrast Every color has 4.5:1 against the surfaces it is set on in the standard modes, and 7:1 in the high-contrast modes. Headings of 24px and over count as large text, but are held to the text ratio anyway.
Minimum size No text style is smaller than 12px. The token build refuses one that is.

Keyboard

Section titled “Keyboard”
Keys Action
Tab Passes over text and headings: neither takes focus. Screen readers move from heading to heading with their own commands.

Internationalization

Section titled “Internationalization”
Concern Behavior
Right to left Text aligns to the start edge, which is the right in right-to-left layouts. Nothing is aligned to the left or the right.
Direction A paragraph takes its direction from its first strong character: on the web when the app sets dir=“auto”, and by default on iOS and Android. A name or a message inside a sentence does not: the punctuation and numbers beside it join it, as in “Message from 2 , minutes ago”. The app isolates it: on the web, in a Text as a span with dir=“auto”; on iOS and Android, between U+2068 FIRST STRONG ISOLATE and U+2069 POP DIRECTIONAL ISOLATE, which BidiFormatter.unicodeWrap also adds on Android.
Long text Text and headings wrap, and grow taller. Only Text with max-lines is cut short, with an ellipsis.
Scripts Line height 1.4 for headings and labels, and 1.5 for body text and captions, clears stacked Vietnamese diacritics. Chinese, Japanese, and Korean use system fonts. Lines break by each language’s rules: strictly for Japanese, and between whole words for Korean. On the web the language is read from lang; on Android it is the person’s, and Korean needs Android 13; iOS breaks Korean between words by itself, and Japanese by its own rules.
Emphasis Emphasis is set in semibold, never in italics: Chinese, Japanese, and Korean faces have no italics.

Guidelines

Section titled “Guidelines”

When to use

Section titled “When to use”

Use Text for running text, labels, and short facts, and Heading for the title of a screen or of a part of it. Set every piece of text in one of them, so that its size, weight, and color come from the theme and grow with the person’s text size setting.

A component that has text of its own, such as a Button, sets it itself. Do not wrap it in Text.

Choosing a heading’s level

Section titled “Choosing a heading’s level”

The level is the heading’s place in the outline of the screen. Assistive technology lists headings by level, and people move from one to the next with it.

  • Choose the level for the heading’s place in the outline, then its look if the default does not fit. Setting variant changes the look and keeps the level.
  • Do not skip levels: a level 4 heading follows a level 3 one.
  • Give a screen one level 1 heading: its title.

By default the look follows the level:

Level Look
1 heading-xl
2 heading-lg
3 heading-md
4–6 heading-sm

Color

Section titled “Color”
  • Use default for most text, and muted for text that supports it, such as a caption.
  • Use danger for an error, or for text about something destructive. Say what is wrong in words: color alone does not reach everyone.
  • Headings take default or muted. There is no danger heading.

Writing

Section titled “Writing”
  • Set emphasis in semibold, not in italics. Chinese, Japanese, and Korean faces have no italics.
  • Keep running text under about 70 characters a line. Give the paragraph a maximum width, not a smaller style.
  • Use sentence case for headings, as for labels.

Long text and truncation

Section titled “Long text and truncation”

Text and headings wrap and grow taller. Nothing is cut short unless you ask for it.

  • Do not truncate headings, labels, or anything a person must read to decide. A heading has no max-lines.
  • Truncate only text that is repeated elsewhere, such as the preview of a message. Assistive technology reads the whole text, but a person reading the screen sees only its start.
  • Leave room for translation: text in other languages can be about 40% longer.

Text from people

Section titled “Text from people”

Names, messages, and other text that people write can be in any language, in either direction.

  • A whole paragraph takes its direction from its first strong character. On the web, set dir="auto" on the Text that holds it. iOS and Android do this by default.
  • A name inside a sentence must be isolated by the app. Without that, the punctuation and numbers beside it join it: “Message from مريم الحسيني, 2 minutes ago” is drawn as “Message from 2 ,مريم الحسيني minutes ago”. This happens on every platform, also to a string built from a translation with the name filled in.
Platform Isolate the name
Web <Text as="span" dir="auto">{name}</Text> inside the sentence
iOS "\u{2068}\(name)\u{2069}": U+2068 first strong isolate, U+2069 pop directional isolate
Android "⁨$name⁩", or BidiFormatter.getInstance().unicodeWrap(name)

Do and don’t

Section titled “Do and don’t”
Do Don’t
Pick the level from the outline, then the look Pick a level for its size
Let headings and labels wrap Truncate a heading or a label with an ellipsis
Use semibold for emphasis Use italics, or color alone, for emphasis
Use caption for a timestamp under a row Use caption for a paragraph
